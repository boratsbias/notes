# Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners (2019)

**Authors:** Alec Radford, Jeffrey Wu, Rewon Child, David Luan, Dario Amodei, Ilya Sutskever
**Area:** Natural Language Processing, Language Modeling
**Link:** [OpenAI](https://openai.com/research/better-language-models)

## What the paper argues

GPT-1 required task-specific fine-tuning. GPT-2 asks: can a large enough language model perform tasks it was never explicitly trained on, just from its pre-training distribution? The answer is yes. Natural text contains examples of translation, summarization, and QA written by humans. A model trained to predict text implicitly learns to do these tasks.

## Scale and data

GPT-2 scales GPT-1 by roughly 10x: 1.5 billion parameters, 48 layers. Training data is WebText, 40 GB of web pages linked from Reddit posts with 3+ upvotes, chosen as a proxy for quality and diversity. The architecture is the same decoder-only transformer with minor changes (layer norm moved to input of each sub-layer, final layer norm added after last block).

## Zero-shot task performance

With no fine-tuning, GPT-2 performs tasks by prompting in natural language:

```
Translation:  "English: {sentence}  French:"         →  continue with translation
Summarization: "{article}  TL;DR:"                   →  continue with summary
QA:           "{context}  Q: {question}  A:"         →  continue with answer
```

The model generates continuations; the task format is implicit in the prompt. This is the earliest demonstration of zero-shot task performance at meaningful quality.

## Results and impact

State of the art on 7 of 8 language modeling benchmarks. Demonstrated that scale alone pushes capability in ways not predictable from smaller models. OpenAI staged its release, which brought significant public attention. Established zero-shot prompting as a research direction that GPT-3 would push much further.
