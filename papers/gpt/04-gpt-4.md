# GPT-4 Technical Report (2023)

**Authors:** OpenAI
**Area:** Natural Language Processing, Large Language Models
**Link:** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774)

## What the paper argues

GPT-4 is a large multimodal model (image + text in, text out) that represents a step change in reasoning capability over GPT-3.5. The report is deliberately sparse on architecture and training details. Its main contribution is the evaluation framework: systematic testing on professional human exams, adversarial prompts, and safety benchmarks, establishing a new standard for how to evaluate large language models.

## Human exam evaluations

GPT-4 is evaluated on standardized professional exams taken as-is:

```
Bar exam (Uniform):      ~90th percentile
SAT Reading:             ~93rd percentile
GRE Quantitative:        ~80th percentile
AP Calculus BC:          ~4/5
USMLE (medical):         passes
```

These scores are reported against human test-taker distributions, making capability claims more concrete than benchmark numbers alone.

## RLHF and alignment

GPT-4 is fine-tuned with RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback): human labelers rank model outputs, a reward model is trained on these rankings, and PPO is used to optimize the language model against the reward model. A KL divergence penalty from the original model prevents the policy from drifting too far from natural language:

```
reward = r_θ(prompt, response) - β · KL(π_RL || π_SFT)
```

## Predictable scaling

The report describes using a smaller proxy model to predict GPT-4's final performance before completing training, enabling compute-efficient decisions about training runs.

## Results and impact

GPT-4 substantially outperformed GPT-3.5 on hard reasoning tasks. It became the foundation for ChatGPT Plus, Bing Chat, and many third-party products. The report's secrecy about model size and architecture sparked significant debate about norms in AI research publication.
