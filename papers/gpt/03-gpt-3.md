# Language Models are Few-Shot Learners (2020)

**Authors:** Tom B. Brown et al.
**Area:** Natural Language Processing, Few-Shot Learning
**Link:** [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)

## What the paper argues

Fine-tuning requires labeled data and a separate trained model per task. GPT-3 argues that a large enough language model can learn new tasks directly from a few examples placed in the prompt, with no gradient updates at all. The model uses the examples as implicit in-context demonstrations.

## Scale

175 billion parameters, trained on a filtered mixture of Common Crawl, WebText2, Books1, Books2, and Wikipedia. Roughly 100x larger than GPT-2. The paper's central empirical finding is that few-shot performance scales smoothly with model size: bigger models are dramatically better few-shot learners.

## Three evaluation settings

```
Zero-shot:  [task description]  →  query
One-shot:   [task description]  [example]  →  query
Few-shot:   [task description]  [ex1] [ex2] ... [exK]  →  query  (K ≤ ~50)
```

No weight updates in any of these. All learning happens in the forward pass over the context window. Larger models benefit more from additional examples.

## What in-context learning implies

The model does not update its weights to "learn" from the examples. Instead, the examples shift the model's completion distribution by demonstrating the input/output format and the task. This is a form of pattern recognition over the context, not gradient-based learning.

## Results and impact

State of the art on TriviaQA, translation, arithmetic, and word unscrambling in the few-shot setting. GPT-3's capabilities and its API-based release shifted the field toward large language models as general-purpose systems. It established scaling laws and in-context learning as central topics in NLP research.
